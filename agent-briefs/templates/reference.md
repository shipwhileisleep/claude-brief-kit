# Reference first (before any work that is judged by quality)

> Use this before music, sound, visuals, writing style or anything where "good" is a matter of quality.
> "Make it sound professional" is not a target. A measured gap to a real example is.
> This step was missing in the ASHEN HUNTER music brief, and the owner called the result amateur.

## Step 1: collect 2 to 3 references
Real, finished examples of what "good" means here, chosen or approved by the owner.
*Two released boss themes in the same genre and tempo range.*
Write down why each one was chosen.

## Step 2: measure the references
Pick 4 to 8 dimensions that a script can measure, and measure the references and the current version the same way.
*Sound: loudness (LUFS), loudness range, true peak, energy above 4 kHz, stereo width, number of layers.*
*Picture: brightness, contrast, share of dark pixels, colour palette, frames where the subject is visible.*
*Text: sentence length, reading level, share of passive voice.*

## Step 3: write the gap table
| Dimension | Reference 1 | Reference 2 | Ours now | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *energy above 4 kHz vs total* | *-14 dB* | *-12 dB* | *-27 dB* | *-15 to -12 dB* |

*The reference numbers in this row are made up for illustration. "Ours now" is real: the first game music had its energy above 4 kHz 27.4 dB below the total.*

The Target column becomes the Requirements of the brief (brief.md).

## Step 4: prototype one piece
Rebuild one piece only, with a time budget, and compare it to the references and to the old version.
Ask the owner to look or listen (the agent cannot judge taste). Only then rebuild the rest the same way.

## Step 5: carry it forward
Put the reference files and the gap table into the "Reference" section of every later brief for this kind of work.
